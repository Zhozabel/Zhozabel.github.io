A lone figure trudged slowly up the steep mountain slope. Biting snow was whipped into her face by a fierce wind,
stinging her skin and turning an already grueling climb into sheer agony.

Hardly anyone would have come here of their own free will. But fate had decreed otherwise — with irony, almost with
cruelty, bestowing upon the girl the gift of the Voice. Instead of a quiet life, she was handed a different destiny: to
save Skyrim from dragons.

Few knew her name, and even those who did simply called her Dovahkiin.

A power granted from above had become her curse — stripping away her home, turning her into a wanderer, leaving only an
endless road and enemies in her wake. The weight of this burden pressed heavier than any weapon she carried. Somewhere
deep inside, beneath heavy armor, a once-sensitive heart ached with a dull, throbbing pain, slowly freezing over,
hardening like everything else in these harsh lands.

A wall of whirling snow reared up, blinding her view. Howling winds twisted the blizzard into tight spirals, until at
last she lost her balance and dropped to her knees. The fierce Nord struggled back to her feet. Inside her chest,
resentment, love, and hatred all warred together, coiling into a tight knot ready to burst. But she knew she could not
yield to weakness.

Straightening her back, she drew in a deep breath of ice-cold air. The Shout tore from the very depths of her soul,
sundering the silence and shaking the heavens:

"LOK VAH KOOR!"

Mountain ridges trembled, the air rang like a taut string, and in an instant the blizzard scattered, clearing the path
to the summit of the Throat of the World.

Dovahkiin remembered Esbern and Delphine’s words — that the dragon had to die. That was her duty as a Dragonborn, her
sole purpose. And yet, something inside her fiercely resisted the thought.
Every new step proved heavier than the last. Behind her back, heavy weapons clattered against armor, dragging down her
exhausted body.

At last, the Word Wall loomed ahead, etched with ancient Dragon runes. Dovahkiin let out a breath of relief as her boots
touched flat stone. Her warm breath dissolved instantly into the freezing air.

"Dovahkiin… you come to the summit once more."

The voice echoed from above — deep, layered, resonating as if from everywhere at once. The air quivered beneath heavy
wingbeats, and Paarthurnax descended onto the stone. Slowly, with quiet majesty. Snow scattered beneath his talons. His
old body was scarred, yet his gaze held neither malice nor threat.

Flinching for only a second, Dovahkiin reached behind her back and drew her battleaxe. Her knuckles turned white on the
haft.

The dragon lowered his head.

"I have waited for you, Dovahkiin." He paused, studying her intently. "I feel it… faas. Fear. But not of me." His voice
softened. "You were brought here by the words of men."

A subtle shift of his leathery wings stirred a cloud of snow dust.

"I know why you have come. They wish for you to kill me. And perhaps… they are right."

The girl tightened her grip on her weapon. Paarthurnax continued:

"Zu’u dovah. I am a dragon. My nature is power… and destruction." He turned his gaze toward the horizon. "But every dov
may choose: to yield to it, or to overcome it. Tell me, Dovahkiin… what is better: to be born good, or to overcome your
evil nature through great effort?"

The silence between them stretched taut, like a chord suspended before resolution.

"Krosis… if my end is your choice…" The dragon rested his head on the snow, prepared to accept death without a fight. "
Let it be your choice alone."

The Nord stood motionless, unable to tear her gaze away from the ancient dragon. The battleaxe hung poised above his
head — one more second, and the lethal strike would descend.

But something in her chest seized painfully once more — sharp, acute, almost unbearable. Something that held her back
from taking that final step.

With a low growl, Dovahkiin hurled the weapon aside. The axe tumbled over the precipice, vanishing into the white void
below. The girl collapsed to her knees before Paarthurnax, and the tears she had held back for so long finally broke
free. They trickled down her weathered face, washing away exhaustion, pain, rage, and guilt. She threw her head back
toward the sky and let out a scream — loud, raw, pouring all her despair and helplessness into that single cry.
She could not kill him. Not after everything. Not him.

The feeling within her hardened heart had not died. Despite the cold, despite her duty, despite the commands of others,
it lived on — strange, unlike love for family, unlike simple affection. Something deeper, something purer. And it bound
her stronger than any oath.

The dragon gently lowered his snout and touched her face.

"Drem yol lok, Dovahkiin… peace." His voice was soft, almost gentle. "Why shed useless tears upon the snows of the
Throat of the World?"

Dovahkiin sobbed and cupped his snout in her hands, pressing her forehead against the cold scales. Her shoulders shook
with ragged weeping.

"Krosis… Paarthurnax…" The words barely squeezed past her throat. "I’m sorry… Zu’u… I can’t…"
Her trembling fingers slid along his snout, tenderly and carefully stroking the rough hide.
Paarthurnax said nothing. He simply lowered one wing slightly, shielding her from the freezing wind.

Through the mist and swirling snow atop High Hrothgar, two figures could barely be distinguished — dragon and mortal, frozen side by side as if plucked from time itself.
The snow fell thicker, veiling them from the world, while the wind carried only a single word into the abyss:

Oblaan.
